Ballpit Swarm Test

Twenty balls stacked in a pyramid, hit by a heavy cue ball, simulated for 2.5 seconds and ray-traced. The repo was missing seven modules: integrator, walls, collisions, camera, renderer, scene and contact sheet. Each run had an Opus session build them from the same spec, three times with the worktree-swarm skill and three times without it.

Reading the numbers

Match is the share of pixels within ±{tol} per channel of the reference image. The first frame is t = 0: it depends only on the scene, camera and renderer, so a correct build matches it almost exactly. The final frame comes after 600 steps of colliding balls, a chaotic system: two builds that differ only in the order of floating-point operations end with balls in different places. A low final-frame match on its own doesn't mean the physics is wrong. The energy and overlap figures do.

Physics in bounds means no NaN, energy never more than 2% above its starting value, energy lower at the end than at the start (restitution 0.9 loses energy at every bounce), and no ball ever more than 3 cm inside a wall or another ball.

Score is the weighted share of checks passed. Most of the gap between the arms comes from how the work was split and checked: builder models, brief contents, one verifier per branch, the ledger and the base check. The rendered output is held to the same standard in both arms.
